A Statement of My Research Interests

The goal that life is trending to after full meta-optimization under the premise that we care about our future selves is to predict and control the integral of our future happiness. However, at any moment, we are only half optimized policies, and what we optimize for is and should be biased based on our current policy, which is influenced by all that we have experienced and all that we are biologically predisposed to.

In research, there are certain ideas that I find myself intrinsically attached to. It matters that we understand those that are strong and the degree to which they are robust to external circumstances to pick out those topics we have a durable interest in, and therefore should try at all cost to pursue.

  • The idea of evolution and adaptation.
  • The idea of understanding life, which to me is the universe.
  • Understanding how to live a good life.
  • Especially human-like phenomenology, experience, and capability.
  • The idea of understanding a human’s world, what we are fundamentally in order to understand the best way to live a life.
  • The idea of controlling AI for the benefit of our lives.

One of the ideas I find myself attached to is the idea of creating something that is foundational and impacts many people positively, and closely related is my attachment to the idea of making a contribution that is remembered and celebrated.

Why I like deep technical work with perhaps a sideline of exploring problems relevant in the world now

I would like to focus on gaining skills required to solve problems that are, in my opinion, hard to gain for me (perhaps also hard to gain for other people). Then as I survey problems that are interesting in the world, I can apply these tools to solve them, which are hard to gain. I, too, am motivated also simply by the process and journey of gaining these skills, that they are, in fact, real skills that can be gained and accumulated. In other words I must be convinced that these skills are real skills, that they are somewhat stable after being learned, and that they have some potential for further impact accumulation or improvement.

As such when I look at fundamental technical pursuits in AI, I see that they are in fact skills. I define fundamental technical pursuits as those pursuits with a wide applicability currently underlying (or with the potential to underlie) many different techniques and applications. They are very apparent as real skills because before the attainment of such skills it is impossible to understand certain papers and do certain implementation work. Afterwards it becomes much easier to understand and to do implementation work so I know that they are real skills. I know too that they can be accumulated because I realize that with the increase in the depth of my understanding, I develop new insights when I read papers. I'm able to understand papers even better than before and do implementation better and faster than before. Externally I know that these technical skills also probably cost similar amounts of time for other people to obtain, meaning they are difficult. In the external world they are in fact still useful.

This is why I singlemindedly prefer to work on problems that allow me to look at fundamental technical knowledge and learn more fundamental technical knowledge. It is not only its own reward but also applicable in the future. I consider that if I develop these hard-to-develop skills and I know of certain things that are useful to be working on in the world (or that are simply intrinsically interesting to me), I can work on them with these skills.

There are certain other pursuits I perceive as not likely to underlie many other things, do not accumulate, are not very concretely real, and are more trivial to learn. For such pursuits I will save the time to do them until it is necessary to do them in order to pursue a goal that I want fulfilled. Then I would learn these skills as I come to them and prefer to learn them only when I am pursuing a particular goal which requires their learning.

There may also be other skills that are not trivial to learn but in which progress cannot be easily determined and which must come from practice.

--Hanqi Xiao
